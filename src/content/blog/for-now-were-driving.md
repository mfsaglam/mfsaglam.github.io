For the first time in history, we have intelligence that isn't organic. It's artificial and human-made. Computers can now think, faster and more efficiently than we can in some areas, coding being the obvious one. For developers and engineers, this changes everything.

I like to think of it as the Tony Stark effect. Cinema predicted this: a human talks to an AI, tells it what to design, and it gets built for real. Jarvis moments are no longer fiction. They're happening right now, and the tools get more capable every week. I don't know what next month looks like, but I believe in this technology. I don't think it's a bubble, and I don't think it will fail. I'm using it today, and it's real.

But here's the thing: it's available to everyone, and what you get out of it depends entirely on you.

AI doesn't come up with your idea for you. It can suggest approaches, but it isn't an expert in your problem. If you blindly follow every suggestion, I doubt you'll end up with what you wanted in the first place. At some point, you'll get stuck. For now, a human is still in control. You spend energy prompting, reading and understanding its responses, and making decisions. It isn't separate from you.

That's why I say it multiplies what you already have. If you know how to build things, it makes you far more powerful. If you don't know what to build or how, it's useless. You can create something great with it, or something garbage, or you may not even know how to use it properly. Talking to it feels easy, but it isn't easy once you start.

Every instruction you give and every decision you make adds up, and those compound effects eventually become the product. Even if you're a "vibe coder" who doesn't check the code, you are still the one steering. You're not being helped by the tool; you're helping it.

That's the key point: **the product is the compound result of your decisions.**

**For now, we're the ones in the driver's seat.**

But will it stay that way? At some point, AI agents may start talking to each other, building things in ways we can't follow, maybe even can't understand. Will we still be steering then, or just passengers?

I don't know. Nobody does. We'll have to wait and see.

*Today, you're helping the machine build. Tomorrow, will the machine still need you to?*
